I am Antsa Sarobidy Randrianantenaina, a soil scientist and ecologist from Madagascar. I trained through a double Master's program between the **University of Antananarivo** (Tropical Agriculture and Sustainable Development, ESSA) and **Sorbonne Université** (Biodiversity, Ecology and Evolution).

My research aims to understand and predict how soils store carbon and sustain ecosystem functioning in tropical landscapes under global change. I combine **Earth observation**, **machine learning** and **spatio-temporal statistical modelling** to advance **digital soil mapping** and the spatial prediction of soil properties and carbon stocks, from open ecosystems and agroforestry systems to blue carbon in mangroves.

At finer scales, I study soil carbon and nitrogen biogeochemistry, soil organic matter dynamics and organo-mineral interactions. I am particularly interested in how land management shapes soil carbon, and in soil–plant–atmosphere feedbacks in tropical and semi-arid agroecosystems. Through precision agriculture and UAV-based remote sensing, I also seek to turn these approaches into robust evidence for sustainable land management and climate policy.

Current work
------
- **Soil carbon in open ecosystems (Taneti Project):** updating Madagascar's national soil organic carbon map with machine learning and Earth observation, with the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew and the Laboratory of Radio-Isotopes, University of Antananarivo.
- **Mangrove carbon stocks:** national-scale modelling of soil carbon and biomass in Madagascar's mangroves to support blue carbon and REDD+ efforts.
- **Agroforestry and soil carbon (FAO & The Nature Conservancy):** quantifying IPCC stock change factors for soil carbon in agroforestry systems.
- **Precision agriculture (Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences):** UAV-based crop mapping and cotton yield estimation.
